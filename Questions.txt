Why is the function called RealSHT if the output is complex?
In my experiments, the imaginary part was necessary to reconstruct the original data!
How should this be handled?
-> It is called realSHT because it expects the input to be real, which speeds up computation (similar to SVD for symmetric matrices).
The output is complex and must be dealt with in this way. While there is rudimentary complex dtype support in pytorch,
for more complex models (transformer) this might not work. Solution: tensor.view_as_real(), which stacks real and imaginary part along a new dimension.
