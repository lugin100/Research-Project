Own Summary of Background Reading Papers
----------------------------------------

Earth system models (ESMs) can forecast climate trends, but are computationally limited to comparatively low spatial resolutions.
Higher resolution is required to study e.g. the effects of climate change. The idea of downscaling is to feed the ESM predictions into a model that upscales the resolution of the predictions.

For this, a number of methods have been proposed:

-- Diffusion models:
Interpret the input as noise, from which the denoised image shall be reconstructed. This is done by training a network to predict a denoised version from the training data with noise added, and punishing deviations from the original data. The network can be for example an U-Net (Convolutions) or a Transformer (DiT).
Drawback: Inference requires many network evaluations to numerically integrate the diffusion process differential equation.

-- Autoregressive models:
An autoregressive model is a model for data that has some kind of sequence X_i.
It predicts the next datum as X_i = f(theta, X_:i-1, epsilon).
Here, theta are the model parameters, X_:i-1 are all previous data and epsilon is random noise.
For example, LLMs use autoregressive modelling to predict the next token in a sequence of tokens via masked transformers.

--- Visual Autoregression (VAR):
An autoregressive model that predicts in each step an image with higher resolution than the last step in order to upscale the image.
